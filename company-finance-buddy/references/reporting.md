# Reporting

The MIS pack, schedules, and how to present figures that aren't final.

---

## The rule that governs all of it

**Every report states its basis.**

Period · what's included · what's still open · what's estimated rather than actual.

A P&L that doesn't disclose that three material accruals are provisional invites decisions on numbers that will move. That's not a formatting preference — it's the difference between a report and a trap.

```
Management Accounts — August 2024
Basis: provisional · 4 items open (₹4,82,000) · 3 accruals estimated
Prepared [date] · not audited · not reviewed by CA
```

**Header goes on every page.** People circulate single pages.

---

## The MIS pack

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Summary** | Revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, PAT — actual vs prior vs budget |
| **P&L** | Line detail, three-column comparative |
| **Balance sheet** | With prior period |
| **Cash flow** | Direct or indirect, stated which |
| **Debtor ageing** | Bucketed, with top exposures |
| **Creditor ageing** | Same |
| **Schedules** | Supporting every material line |
| **Variance notes** | Everything outside the threshold, explained |
| **Open items** | With owner and age |
| **GST and TDS status** | Reconciled position, ITC at risk |

**Summary first, one page.** Most readers stop there. If the important thing is on page 7, it hasn't been communicated.

## Variance narrative

**A variance without an explanation is an open question dressed as a report.**

Set a threshold — commonly the greater of a percentage and an absolute value, so small accounts don't trigger on noise and large ones don't hide movement.

| Weak | Useful |
|---|---|
| "Professional fees increased" | "Professional fees ₹4.82L vs ₹1.20L average — statutory audit fee ₹3.50L booked in August, annual" |
| "Revenue below budget" | "Revenue ₹42L vs ₹50L budget. Two client invoices totalling ₹9L slipped to September on delivery timing." |
| "Repairs up significantly" | "Repairs ₹2.10L vs ₹0.34L average — **unexplained, with Ops**" |

**"Unexplained" is a legitimate and necessary entry.** Better than inventing a plausible cause. Flag it, name the owner, carry it to open items.

## Presenting estimates

**Never let an estimate look computed.**

Mark it in the schedule, in the narrative, and in the total:

```
ACCRUALS — August

Audit fee              ₹3,50,000   actual, invoice received
Electricity            ₹  42,000   estimated · 3-month average
Legal — Sharma & Co    ₹1,20,000   estimated · engagement letter, no invoice
Freight                ₹  38,000   actual, GRN matched
                       ──────────
                       ₹5,50,000   of which ₹1,62,000 estimated
```

That last line is the one that matters. A reader seeing ₹5,50,000 alone treats it as fact.

## Schedules

Every material balance sheet line needs one. The schedule is what makes the number checkable.

**Minimum:** opening, additions, deletions, closing, and a tie to the ledger.

```
FIXED ASSETS — August

              Opening    Additions   Disposals   Depn      Closing
Plant         42,80,000   2,40,000          —   (71,333)  44,48,667
Computers      8,20,000     65,000    (40,000)  (22,750)   8,22,250
Furniture      3,10,000          —          —   ( 5,167)   3,04,833
              ─────────   ─────────   ────────  ────────  ─────────
              54,10,000   3,05,000    (40,000) (99,250)   55,75,750

Ties to GL 1200-1290 ✓
Depreciation: SLM at rates per company policy
```

**Always show the tie.** A schedule that doesn't reconcile to the ledger is a separate document, not support.

## Ageing

Bucket consistently: current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+.

**Show concentration.** Total ageing hides risk. Ten crore of debtors is fine unless six crore of it is one customer at 90+ days.

```
DEBTOR AGEING — August

              Current    1-30      31-60     61-90     90+       Total
Total       62,40,000  18,20,000  8,40,000  3,10,000  6,80,000  98,90,000
                  63%       18%        8%        3%        7%

90+ CONCENTRATION
  Meridian Systems   ₹4,20,000   142 days   ← 62% of the 90+ bucket
  Cortez Retail      ₹1,80,000   118 days
```

## Cash flow

**State whether it's direct or indirect.** They answer different questions and readers assume different things.

**Reconcile to the actual movement in bank balances.** A cash flow that doesn't tie to the bank is arithmetic, not a cash flow.

---

## What not to do

**Don't present provisional as final.** The most common and most damaging reporting error.

**Don't bury the bad number.** If margin fell, it goes in the summary, not on page 9.

**Don't over-round.** ₹42.8L is fine in a summary. The schedule needs the rupees.

**Don't mix bases without saying so.** Some lines actual, some estimated, with no marking, produces a report nobody can rely on and nobody can audit.

**Don't editorialise on causes you don't know.** "Revenue down due to market conditions" when you don't know that is an invented explanation, and it stops someone finding the real one.

**Don't assert compliance.** "GST filings are up to date" is a compliance conclusion. "GSTR-1 and 3B filed for July per portal acknowledgements; August 3B pending" is a fact.

---

## Distinguishing the four registers

Visibly, always:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Actual** | Invoiced, banked, or otherwise documented |
| **Accrued** | Incurred, not invoiced — with the basis stated |
| **Estimated** | Provisional — with the method stated |
| **Provisional** | Subject to a pending reconciliation or decision |

Collapsing these is how a provisional figure becomes a board number three weeks later.
